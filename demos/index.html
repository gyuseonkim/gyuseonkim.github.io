Overview

This demonstration replays an evaluation episode of a reinforcement learning (RL) policy that autonomously flies a military unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) from a launch point to a designated ground target. The mission takes place over procedurally generated terrain defended by an integrated air-defense network of surveillance radars, a surface-to-air missile (SAM) site, and electronic jammers, together with tall vertical obstacles. The trained actor network commands the UAV throughout the sortie, balancing progress toward the target against the adversary's probability of detection and radar track quality. The mission proceeds through ingress, midcourse, and terminal phases and concludes with a terminal dive onto the target.

Scenario Configuration

Terrain 8 km × 8 km procedurally generated digital elevation model (110 × 110 grid; elevation −30 to 390 m)
Air-defense threats 5 surveillance radars (coverage radius 2.8–3.0 km), 1 SAM site (engagement radius 0.66 km), 2 electronic jammers (0.77–1.38 km)
Obstacles 4 vertical obstacles, 145–219 m in height
Mission Launch-to-target strike along a 9-waypoint reference route (8.4 km straight-line distance)
Monitored states Airspeed, altitude above ground level (AGL), attitude, P(det), radar track quality, and mission phase

Episode Results

Mission outcome Success
Flight time / distance 415 s / 9.35 km (1.12× the straight-line distance)
Airspeed 22.5 m/s on average (18.1–34.8 m/s)
Altitude 90 m AGL on average
Radar exposure 100% of the trajectory lies within the nominal coverage of at least one radar
Probability of detection Mean 0.09, peak 0.87; P(det) > 0.5 in only 1.7% of time steps
SAM avoidance Closest approach 787 m, outside the 660 m engagement radius
Terminal accuracy 3.8 m horizontal miss distance

Mission Phases

Phase Time window Mean AGL Mean P(det)
