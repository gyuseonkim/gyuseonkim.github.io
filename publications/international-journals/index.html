  1. IEEE IoT-J
    Exponentially Scalable Quantum Reinforcement Learning for Large-Scale Network Scheduling
    Gyu Seon Kim, Emily Jimin Roh, Soohyun Park, and 1 more author
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, 2026
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    Parameterized-quantum-circuit learning has attracted growing interest, and quantum reinforcement learning (QRL) is especially promising for faster computation. Its reliance on quantum measurements for producing outputs nevertheless creates a severe scalability problem when an application has a very large output space. Conventional QRL designs can also require a rapidly growing qubit register to produce high-dimensional outputs, which is especially burdensome for noisy intermediate-scale quantum hardware. The paper introduces exponentially scalable QRL (ES-QRL), which uses basis measurement to support large-scale network scheduling with extremely high-dimensional outputs. This measurement design overcomes the restriction imposed by a small qubit count, allowing the model to train over an exponentially large output space while using only a few qubits. Evaluations on network-scheduling tasks with numerous output dimensions show that ES-QRL performs better than the compared classical RL and existing QRL frameworks.

  2. IEEE TMC
    Scale-Reconfigurable Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Aerial Networks in Dynamic Environments
    Gyu Seon Kim, Emily Jimin Roh, Soyi Jung, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing, 2026
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    Large constellations of CubeSats and deployments of many UAVs are needed to build realistic aerial access networks with global coverage. In such a system, each ground station must continually determine which non-terrestrial devices to select and from which devices it will receive network service. The number and type of reachable devices change over time, producing uncertain dynamic topologies that conflict with conventional reinforcement-learning models whose supported device count is fixed during both training and inference. To solve this practical mismatch, the paper proposes scale-reconfigurable multi-agent reinforcement learning (SR-MARL). Its flexible neural networks alter their architecture according to the current mix and population of CubeSats and UAVs within each ground station’s communication range, enabling adaptive station management and control. In the reported evaluation, SR-MARL achieves 1.64 times the average network performance and 2.39 times the average residual device energy of the benchmark methods.

  3. IEEE IoT-J
    Learning-based Resilient Dynamic Routing for Fault-Tolerant Robust LEO Satellite Networks
    Gyu Seon Kim, Chaemoon Im, Yeong Goo Kim, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, 2026
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    Geographic and economic barriers, together with disruptions caused by disasters and warfare, make seamless and resilient global Internet access difficult. Satellite communication can bridge those gaps, although the altitude of conventional GEO systems produces inherent latency. LEO satellites reduce propagation delay and can improve network performance, but their high orbital speed continually changes both topology and inter-satellite-link availability. Conventional routing reacts poorly to those changes and to congestion, leading to inefficient path choices and greater delay. The proposed adaptive reinforcement-learning non-terrestrial-network routing algorithm, ARL-NTNR, continually interacts with the evolving constellation to learn and update its routing policy in real time. By balancing shortest-path decisions against current queue backlogs, it responds to congestion and is reported to reduce packet delay and packet loss.

  4. IEEE ComMag
    Quantum Learning for Autonomous Aircraft Control: A Reinforcement Learning Perspective
    Gyu Seon Kim, Jaehyun Chung, Soohyun Park, and 3 more authors
    IEEE Communications Magazine, 2026
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    Aircraft attitude control must maintain stability under disturbances such as turbulence and vortices. Although reinforcement-learning controllers can adapt to uncertain dynamics, the number of trainable parameters and the resulting computation can prevent their practical deployment on airborne systems. This work replaces the classical neural network in an actor-critic attitude controller with a quantum neural network, using superposition and entanglement to create a computationally efficient and lightweight QRL controller. The method is tested in a simulated flight-control environment based on the aerodynamic characteristics of a Boeing B777-300X and exposed to stochastic vortical disturbances. The quantum controller reduces the number of training parameters by approximately a factor of 760 relative to its classical-neural-network counterpart while retaining resilient attitude-control behavior, supporting QRL as a parameter-efficient option for aircraft with limited computing resources.

  5. IEEE Network
    Quantum Jump to Connected Worlds: Paradigm Shifts in Mobility and Network Applications via Quantum Neural Networks
    Hyunsoo Lee, Gyu Seon Kim, Emily Jimin Roh, and 4 more authors
    IEEE Network, 2026
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    Quantum computing and artificial intelligence are increasingly being combined in both academic and industrial research, creating a quantum-AI field with potential benefits for mobility and communication networks. This article introduces quantum neural networks and examines how they can be used in distributed networking applications. It first surveys industrial activity in quantum AI and then explains the underlying concepts of quantum computation and quantum neural-network design.   6. IEEE T-IV
    Cooperative Reinforcement Learning for Military Drones over Large-Scale Battlefields
    Gyu Seon Kim, Sungjoon Lee, Taejin Woo, and 1 more author
    IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, 2024
    Early Access
    Abs DOI Bib

    The shift toward autonomous military systems has made cooperative drone operations a central element of modern warfare. Their effectiveness depends on whether an entire swarm can act robustly in a complex, large battlefield while the target position changes dynamically. The control problem therefore combines three objectives: preserving cooperative swarm flight, maximizing coverage of the target bombing zone, and optimizing energy efficiency. The paper applies multi-agent deep reinforcement learning so the drones can coordinate these competing objectives rather than operate independently. To make the assessment representative of a known military platform, the simulations adopt characteristics of the Shahed-136 and use a battlefield volume of 40,000 cubic kilometers. The proposed approach is evaluated in terms of swarm-flight behavior, bombing success rate, and drone energy efficiency under dynamic target conditions.

  1. IEEE TMC
    Joint Sustainable Control and Quantum Reinforcement Learning for Energy-Efficient Cube-Satellite Networks
    Soohyun Park, Gyu Seon Kim, Soyi Jung, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing, vol. 25, no. 7, pp. 10385-10401 , July 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    CubeSats are attractive for global networking and surveillance because several can be positioned together near Earth in a target orbit, but their small batteries limit mission duration. The paper treats orbital control and constellation scheduling as coupled energy-management problems and proposes a two-stage reinforcement-learning framework for operation under uncertainty. In the first stage, deep deterministic policy gradient controls the vertical orbital position of one CubeSat over a continuous action space. The second stage uses quantum multi-agent reinforcement learning with a projection-valued measure (PVM) to coordinate multiple CubeSats while reducing the scheduling-action dimension to logarithmic scale. The authors emphasize that the single-satellite maneuver and multi-satellite scheduling problems cannot be optimized separately when pursuing extreme energy efficiency. Compared with the evaluated baselines, the framework reports 1.51 times higher orbital-control performance, 2.71 times greater converged reward in very large action spaces, and 2.27 times higher average network performance.

  2. IEEE TAES
    Quantum Reinforcement Learning for Joint Control, Communication, and Computing in Stabilized Reusable Space Rocket
    Gyu Seon Kim, Jaehyun Chung, Soyi Jung, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, vol. 62, pp. 3838-3863 , March 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Reusable rockets can lower launch costs and improve space-industry efficiency across repeated missions, but reliable operation throughout the complete mission requires control, communication, and onboard computing to be designed together. The proposed QRL-C3 framework addresses the poor training behavior that conventional reinforcement learning can exhibit in high-dimensional state and action spaces as their dimensions rise. It uses quantum neural networks with superposition and entanglement to improve information representation and processing within the quantum domain. Its control component supports robust reentry and precise landing, while a quantum multi-agent scheduler coordinates seamless communication with several ground stations for Internet-of-Things service. A third component manages image processing and transmission power through queue-aware, energy-efficient computing. These components operate jointly to improve mission performance and reusability. Validation with a SpaceX Falcon 9 model shows higher training efficiency than conventional RL approaches, and the work presents the framework as the first QAI-based integration of all three rocket subsystems for future space missions.

  3. IEEE TAES
    Integrated Control, Communication, and Computing for Mission-Critical Embedded Unmanned Aerial Vehicles
    Gyu Seon Kim, Soohyun Park, Soyi Jung, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, vol. 62, pp. 682-703 , February 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Military UAVs are valued for their agility and ability to penetrate hostile territory, but they remain vulnerable to spoofed GPS signals that deliberately mislead navigation. To support flight when reliable GPS is unavailable, the paper develops a location-estimation method and analyzes UAV aerodynamics for rapid maneuvering and collision prevention. Reconnaissance by a UAV swarm also requires an uninterrupted and reliable communication system that remains robust as the formation moves under mission constraints. The proposed hybrid medium-access-control protocol therefore changes between carrier-sense multiple access and time-division multiple access according to the vehicles’ movement states during swarm flight. Since optimizing communication alone can neglect energy consumption and onboard queue stability, the design adds Lyapunov-based dynamic power control for low-power computing. The resulting embedded UAV platform integrates control, communication, and computing so that the three components operate together during stable reconnaissance missions, and its performance is evaluated in each of these aspects.

  4. IEEE IoT-J
    Hybrid Large Language Models and Reinforcement Learning for Energy-Efficient Multisatellite Scheduling: Boosting the Performance From Scratch
    Hyojun Ahn, Gyu Seon Kim, In-Sop Cho, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, vol. 13, no. 3, pp. 5379-5392 , February 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    LEO constellations offer broad, low-delay connectivity, yet their rapidly changing satellite positions make transmission scheduling difficult. BoostRL combines a large language model with reinforcement learning so that an LLM-generated initial policy supplies informed guidance from the start, overcoming inefficient learning from scratch before the RL agent has acquired useful experience and helping it adapt rapidly to changing network conditions. A hybrid policy gradually transfers decision authority from the language-model recommendations to the autonomous learned policy as training progresses. Tailored Q-value initialization and a modified loss function further align the early optimization process with the LLM’s guidance. Simulations driven by two-line-element orbital data show faster convergence and greater scheduling efficiency, supporting BoostRL as an adaptive and scalable method for satellite communication networks at scale.

  5. IEEE TMC
    Quantum Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Cooperative Mobile Access in Space-Air-Ground Integrated Networks
    Gyu Seon Kim, Yeryeong Cho, Jaehyun Chung, and 4 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing, vol. 25, no. 1, pp. 1200-1218 , January 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    A SAGIN built only with CubeSats can lose sustainable access in areas such as the polar regions and is also constrained by satellite energy. High-altitude long-endurance UAVs can complement both the coverage and energy shortcomings of the constellation. Adding the two platform types, however, causes each ground station’s scheduling-action space to grow with the combined fleet and exposes conventional learning to the curse of dimensionality. The paper proposes a quantum multi-agent reinforcement-learning scheduler that coordinates ground-station access to CubeSats and HALE-UAVs to improve both global availability and energy efficiency. Its quantum formulation reduces the scheduling dimension logarithmically as the number of aerial and space platforms increases. The method also accounts for location-dependent differences in ground-station traffic so that access can be differentiated, and data-intensive experiments in realistic CubeSat and HALE-UAV settings validate its superiority.

  1. IEEE TON
    Slimmable Federated Reinforcement Learning for Energy-Efficient Proactive Caching
    Hankyul Baek, Gyu Seon Kim, Soohyun Park, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Networking, vol. 33, no. 4, pp. 2079-2094 , August 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Deep neural models have increasingly replaced classical proactive-caching algorithms, and enlarging those models can improve capability at the cost of greater storage and computation. Their fixed structures are difficult to deploy in realistic settings where user mobility continually changes the active population and therefore the dimensions of model inputs. Conventional implementations may have to expand the input layer to the largest anticipated size or fill absent values through imputation. SlimFRL integrates slimmable neural networks with federated reinforcement learning so that network width can be adjusted during training according to the current number of users. The slimmable structure also selects a suitable input dimension without imputation, avoiding unnecessary inputs and improving performance in dynamic environments in practice. The paper proposes several SlimFRL training algorithms, supports them with convergence analysis, and validates their advantages in reward and additional cost functions through various experiments.

  2. IEEE TAES
    Quantum Multiagent Reinforcement Learning for Joint Cube Satellites and High-Altitude Long-Endurance Aerial Vehicles in SAGIN
    Gyu Seon Kim, Yeryeong Cho, Soohyun Park, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, vol. 61, no. 4, pp. 9490-9510 , August 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    Providing genuinely global access solely through CubeSats is challenging because frequent handovers and polar-region coverage gaps compromise service availability. The paper therefore coordinates multiple CubeSats with high-altitude long-endurance UAVs through a newly designed quantum multi-agent reinforcement-learning scheduler. This complementary combination is intended to preserve global availability despite topology changes and regional coverage gaps. The algorithm also seeks to improve service quality, energy efficiency, and network capacity across the combined system. A modified quantum-measurement procedure reduces the scheduling-action dimension logarithmically, which becomes increasingly important as the numbers of CubeSats and HALE-UAVs grow across the network. Experiments built from real-world data in a realistic joint-platform environment demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed scheduler.

  3. IEEE IoT-J
    Quantum Reinforcement Learning for Lightweight LEO Satellite Routing
    Gyu Seon Kim, Sungjoon Lee, In-Sop Cho, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, vol. 12, no. 14, pp. 28986-29004 , July 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    LEO satellite networks provide low propagation delay, broad coverage, and rapid deployment, but their changing topology and frequent handovers demand routing that is both adaptive and computationally light. QRL-SR applies quantum reinforcement learning to this problem under the small size, weight, power, and onboard-computing limits of LEO spacecraft. The design addresses three linked requirements: adaptation to a time-varying network, explicit representation of orbital geometry, and a lightweight trainable policy. It converts two-line-element celestial data into orbital coordinates to locate satellites accurately as time advances and uses QRL to reduce the policy’s trainable-parameter count. Its routing objective is to lower free-space path loss, end-to-end delay, and the number of inter-satellite-link hops. Experiments show routing performance that is comparable to or better than conventional algorithms while consuming substantially fewer computational resources.

  4. IEEE IoT-J
    Joint Quantum Reinforcement Learning and Neural Myerson Auction for High-Quality Digital-Twin Services in Multitier Networks
    Soohyun Park, Gyu Seon Kim, and Joongheon Kim
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, vol. 12, no. 13, pp. 23722-23735 , July 2025
    Abs DOI Bib

    The paper develops a two-stage method for delivering high-quality digital-twin services through cloud-assisted multitier networks. First, quantum multi-agent reinforcement learning schedules individually quality-controlled segments of cloud-hosted virtual objects while incorporating age of information for low-latency service. Because each edge can select more segments as the service grows, its action space expands exponentially and becomes difficult for conventional MARL to learn; the quantum-inspired scheduler reduces that decision dimension to logarithmic scale. The second stage provides fast and seamless delivery of the differentiated segments requested by each user through one of the nearby edge nodes. A neural-Myerson second-price auction provides truthful, distributed allocation while realizing low-complexity computation by avoiding NP-hard integer programming. Compared with the evaluated benchmarks, the method reports a high-dimensional reward 8.33 times as large and revenue 1.18 times as large.

  1. IEEE IoT-J
    Markov Decision Policies for Distributed Angular Routing in LEO Mobile Satellite Constellation Networks
    Soohyun Park, Gyu Seon Kim, Soyi Jung, and 1 more author
    IEEE Internet of Things Journal, vol. 11, no. 23, pp. 38744-38754 , December 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    Routing in a time-varying LEO constellation must operate without impractical global centralized computation and should represent satellite motion in orbital coordinates. This work therefore bases every routing decision on distributed angular calculations between satellites. It formulates the process as a Markov decision problem for discrete-time sequential decisions over the changing satellite network and introduces the MDP-based distributed angular routing algorithm, MDAR, for seamless operation. An angular-difference reward guides packets along a path that is geometrically close to a straight line from source to destination in the orbital frame. Evaluation in a realistic dynamic constellation with real two-line-element satellite data shows that MDAR outperforms the comparison methods in routing success rate, reward convergence, and successfully delivered throughput.

  2. IEEE ComMag
    Quantum Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning is All You Need: Coordinated Global Access in Integrated TN/NTN Cube-Satellite Networks
    Soohyun Park, Gyu Seon Kim, Zhu Han, and 1 more author
    IEEE Communications Magazine, vol. 62, no. 10, pp. 86-92 , October 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    This article addresses novel quantum multi-agent reinforcement learning (QMARL)-based scheduling for integrated terrestrial ground-stations and large-scale non-terrestrial cube-satellites networks to enable coordinated global access services. By utilizing quantum-based neural networks for designing QMARL, stable large-scale cube-satellite scheduling can be realized thanks to fast training with quantum-specific learning computation methods. In addition to the benefit from conventional QMARL algorithms, our proposed QMARL-based scheduler has two key characteristics: high-scalability for low-dimensional scheduling, and energy-efficient geometry-awareness. For high-scalability, projection value measure (PVM)-based measurement is proposed for reducing scheduling action dimensions into a logarithmic-scale, which is obviously helpful for stable and fast convergence during QMARL training. Furthermore, energy-efficient geometry-aware operations can be realized by considering the fair energy consumption and positions in cube-satellites, which can be used for differentiated charging strategies. Our evaluation results verify that the proposed low-dimensional geometry-aware QMARL-based scheduler achieves desired performance improvements.

  3. IEEE TVT
    Realizing Stabilized Landing for Computation-Limited Reusable Rockets: A Quantum Reinforcement Learning Approach
    Gyu Seon Kim, Jaehyun Chung, and Soohyun Park
    IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology, vol. 73, no. 8, pp. 12252-12257 , August 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    The advent of reusable rockets has heralded a new era in space exploration, reducing the costs of launching satellites by a significant factor. Traditional rockets were disposable, but the design of reusable rockets for repeated use has revolutionized the financial dynamics of space missions. The most critical phase of reusable rockets is the landing stage, which involves managing the tremendous speed and altitude for safe recovery. The complexity of this task presents new challenges for control systems, specifically in terms of precision and adaptability. Classical control systems like the model predictive control (MPC) controller lack the flexibility to adapt to dynamic system changes, making the re-design of the controller costly and time consuming. This paper explores the integration of quantum reinforcement learning into the control systems of reusable rockets as a promising alternative. Unlike classical reinforcement learning, quantum reinforcement learning uses quantum bits that can exist in superposition, allowing for more efficient information encoding and reducing the number of parameters required. This leads to increased computational efficiency, reduced memory requirements, and more stable and predictable performance. Due to the nature of reusable rockets, which must be light, heavy computers cannot fit into them. In the reusable rocket scenario, quantum reinforcement learning, which has reduced memory requirements due to fewer parameters, is a good solution.

  4. IEEE Access
    Farthest Agent Selection With Episode-Wise Observations for Real-Time Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Applications
    Hyunsoo Lee, Gyu Seon Kim, Minseok Choi, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Access, vol. 12, pp. 108504-108514 , August 2024
    Abs DOI Bib

    Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) algorithms have been widely used for many applications requiring sequential decision-making to maximize the expected rewards through multi-agent cooperation. However, MARL faces significant challenges, particularly in resource-limited real-time computing environments. To tackle this problem, this paper considers the selection of agents for training which can be beneficial in terms of computation-overhead reduction. For the selection, a farthest agent selection (FAS) is proposed, inspired by the farthest point sampling for representative sample selection in 3D point cloud processing. The proposed FAS method is able to choose agents based on their episode-specific observations in real-time. Additionally, the number of selected agents can be determined based on the real-time variances in the observations of each agent. The proposed FAS method is rigorously evaluated using the StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC) and Predator-Prey (PP) tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing MARL algorithms. This research is scalable, and thus, it can contribute to the development of more efficient MARL training methodologies for various applications such as real-time strategy games and human-robot cooperation scenarios requiring multi-agent cooperation under partial observability.

  2. IEEE T-IV
    Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Cooperative Air Transportation Services in City-Wide Autonomous Urban Air Mobility
    Chanyoung Park, Gyu Seon Kim, Soohyun Park, and 2 more authors
    IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, vol. 8, no. 8, pp. 4016-4030 , August 2023
    Abs DOI Bib

    The development of urban-air-mobility (UAM) is rapidly progressing with spurs, and the demand for efficient transportation management systems is a rising need due to the multifaceted environmental uncertainties. Thus, this article proposes a novel air transportation service management algorithm based on multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL) to address the challenges of multi-UAM cooperation. Specifically, the proposed algorithm in this article is based on communication network (CommNet) method utilizing centralized training and distributed execution (CTDE) in multiple UAMs for providing efficient air transportation services to passengers collaboratively. Furthermore, this article adopts actual vertiport maps and UAM specifications for constructing realistic air transportation networks. By evaluating the performance of the proposed algorithm in data-intensive simulations, the results show that the proposed algorithm outperforms existing approaches in terms of air transportation service quality. Furthermore, there are no inferior UAMs by utilizing parameter sharing in CommNet and a centralized critic network in CTDE. Therefore, it can be confirmed that the research results in this article can provide a promising solution for autonomous air transportation management systems in city-wide urban areas.
